## Personal and Housing Context

I am 22 and relocating to San Francisco to begin working as a software engineer at Roblox. My job is based in San Mateo, but only in person Tuesday, Wednesday, and Thursday. I will not own a car. I recently moved to the Bay Area and am temporarily staying with friends near Oak Street and Haight Street while I search for my own apartment.

I originally planned to share a two-bedroom apartment at Vara Apartments near 15th and Mission. After visiting the neighborhood in person, I strongly disliked the immediate surroundings and decided not to move forward. The area around Mission Street felt too dirty, chaotic, and unpleasant for me to feel comfortable living there. I am now searching primarily for a studio or one-bedroom apartment.

## Budget

My preferred maximum rent is approximately $3,500 per month.

A place does not need to be cheap if it is genuinely attractive, comfortable, and in a neighborhood I like. I am willing to pay toward the top of my budget for a strong location and a well-maintained apartment.

## Preferred Neighborhoods

My strongest target areas are:

* Hayes Valley
* Alamo Square
* Lower Haight
* Duboce Triangle
* The Castro
* Areas around Oak Street, Page Street, Haight Street, and the Panhandle
* Some parts of the Lower Pacific Heights, Western Addition, and NoPa area
* Select parts of Nob Hill
* Select parts of the Valencia corridor, provided the immediate block feels pleasant

I particularly like the general area around my friends’ house near Oak and Haight. Being within walking distance of them would be a major advantage.

I am open to nearby neighborhoods when the individual block is attractive, calm, and reasonably clean. Neighborhood names alone should not determine whether a listing qualifies because San Francisco can change substantially from block to block.

## Location Preferences

I care heavily about the immediate surroundings of the building.

Positive signals include:

* Tree-lined or attractive residential streets
* Older San Francisco architecture with character
* Walkability to cafés, restaurants, parks, gyms, and useful stores
* A neighborhood that feels active without feeling chaotic
* Easy access to Hayes Valley, the Castro, Lower Haight, Duboce Park, Alamo Square, or the Panhandle
* Reasonable access to public transportation for commuting toward Caltrain or San Mateo
* Proximity to my friends near Oak and Haight

Negative signals include:

* Blocks that feel dirty, industrial, desolate, or dominated by large roads
* Heavy visible street disorder immediately outside the building
* Locations deep in the Tenderloin
* The immediate 15th and Mission area
* Rougher sections of SoMa
* Apartments surrounded primarily by warehouses, freeway infrastructure, parking lots, or auto-oriented businesses
* A technically convenient commute that comes at the expense of enjoying the neighborhood

I would rather have a somewhat longer commute while living in an area I enjoy than optimize entirely around commute time.

## Apartment Preferences

My ideal apartment is a studio or one-bedroom with a pleasant interior and good natural light.

Strong positive features include:

* Large windows
* Good natural light
* Hardwood floors
* A clean and updated kitchen or bathroom
* A layout that feels like a real living space rather than a tiny hotel room
* Laundry in the building
* A secure and well-maintained entrance
* A quieter residential building
* Attractive architectural details
* Bay windows
* A separate or partially separated kitchen
* Enough room for a bed, desk, and basic living area
* Pet-friendly policies are a mild positive, though not essential
* Air conditioning is a positive but is no longer a major requirement

I am open to older buildings. A charming, well-maintained older apartment can be preferable to a generic new apartment building.

Potential negatives include:

* Very dark interiors
* Ground-floor units with poor privacy
* Extremely small layouts
* Shared bathrooms
* Buildings that look poorly maintained
* Listings with obvious scam signals
* Excessive move-in fees or unusual payment requests
* Furnished short-term rentals presented as normal apartments
* Units where the photos avoid showing the kitchen, bathroom, windows, or building exterior

### 2341 Market Street

The Upper Market, Castro, and Duboce Triangle area is considerably more appealing to me than 15th and Mission. Listings around Market Street should be evaluated based on the exact cross streets.

### Charming Nob Hill Studio

A charming Nob Hill studio can fit my taste when it is on a clean, residential block and has good natural light, architectural character, and reasonable access to transit.

Nob Hill is not my top target compared with Hayes Valley, Alamo Square, Lower Haight, or the Castro, but attractive studios there should still be included.

### 235 Valencia Street

Valencia Street is appealing in principle because of its restaurants, walkability, and access to the Mission and Hayes Valley. However, I am sensitive to the exact section of Valencia and the surrounding blocks.

Listings farther north on Valencia, closer to Market Street and Hayes Valley, may be more appealing than listings closer to the part of the Mission I disliked. The scraper should flag the exact cross streets rather than treating all Valencia listings equally.

## Listings That Should Rank Poorly

Listings similar to the following should be deprioritized:

* A new luxury studio directly around 15th and Mission
* A cheap apartment deep in the Tenderloin
* A modern unit in an industrial section of SoMa
* A dark ground-floor studio with bars over the windows
* A listing with no exact address or only a vague neighborhood label
* A unit requiring payment before an in-person or live video tour
* A listing using unusually low rent to attract applicants
* A room in a shared apartment presented as a private studio
* A short-term sublet when the listing does not clearly state that it is temporary
* A listing whose photos appear stolen, inconsistent, or heavily cropped

## Search Priorities

The scraper should prioritize newly posted listings because desirable San Francisco apartments may receive many inquiries quickly.

The most important initial filters are:

1. Studio or one-bedroom
2. Monthly rent at or below approximately $3,500
3. Located in or near my preferred neighborhoods
4. Posted recently
5. Appears to be a full private apartment rather than a room
6. Includes enough information and photos to assess legitimacy
7. Does not contain obvious scam language

The scraper should preserve borderline listings rather than filtering too aggressively. I would rather manually reject some imperfect results than miss a strong apartment because Craigslist used an unexpected neighborhood name or incomplete description.

## Scam Awareness

Craigslist listings should be considered suspicious when they:

* Ask for deposits or application fees before a tour
* Claim the landlord is out of the country
* Refuse an in-person or live video walkthrough
* Offer rent dramatically below comparable listings
* Request payment through wire transfer, cryptocurrency, gift card, Zelle, or another irreversible method
* Use an email address or phone number that appears unrelated to the owner or property manager
* Reuse the same photos or description for multiple addresses
* Contain inconsistent details about the address, rent, bedrooms, or amenities
* Pressure the applicant to act immediately without normal verification

A low scam-risk score should not mean that a listing is verified. It should only mean that the text lacks common warning signs.
